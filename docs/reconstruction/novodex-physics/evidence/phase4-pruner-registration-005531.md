# Phase 4 Pruner registration: `phys_fn_005531`

The x87 build previously substituted a factory-time process-pool allocation for the call the shipped `Pruner::Pruner` makes at `this+0x34`. That missed a key per-instance effect: the constructor inserts each pruner into the process-wide registry and stores its handle at +0x34; each remove/update advances the timestamp at +0x38; the destructor unregisters the handle.

`Physics/src/PrunerRegistration.cpp` already contained the measured registry implementation but was excluded from the x87 DLL. The reconstruction now uses `NxPrunerRegistration` for both scalar and x87 builds, calls registration from the common base constructor, unregisters from the common base destructor, and routes the successful static/base timestamp increments through the same member. The fake pool initialization in `Scene.cpp` and the engine-factory priming calls are removed. The registry translation unit is included in the x87 DLL and in the three test harnesses that compile `IcePruner.cpp` directly. Public headers were not changed.

`NxPhysicsSceneRaycastTests` inspects the two Pruner instances through the public Scene wrapper and requires both the empty-bound words and exact registration state. After the scene has registered its seven static and four dynamic shapes, the oracle reports static handle/timestamp `00000000.00000006` and dynamic `00000001.00000004`. A focused actual-DLL control matches exactly (`oracle_exit=0`, `candidate_exit=0`, `stdout_delta=0`, `stderr_exact=True`).

The assertion was mutation-tested by omitting only the constructor’s registration call. The candidate then reported poisoned handle/timestamp words and failed, while the oracle remained successful (`oracle_exit=0`, `candidate_exit=1`, `stdout_delta=2793`). After restoring the source, its timestamp was advanced to force MSBuild to recompile it; the DLL and harness were rebuilt, and the focused differential returned to exact. Full gates passed: Phase 4 532/532, Phase 5 2739/2739, Phase 7 2870/2870. The raw logs, binary hashes and per-log hashes are in [the manifest and transcripts](raw/phase4-pruner-registration-005531-20261015/).

The registry’s buffer-allocation failure and capacity-exhaustion branches are not reconstructed by this successful-path proof. The destructor’s handle reuse is implemented but still needs its own observable lifecycle assertion and mutation. Those remain open; no broader completion claim is made.
